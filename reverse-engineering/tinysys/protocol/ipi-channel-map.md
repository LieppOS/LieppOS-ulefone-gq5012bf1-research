Units: `slots` are 32-bit words (`len` argument of `mtk_ipi_send*`). `slot ofs` is the offset in the mailbox SRAM in words. `pin` is the IRQ bit.

## ACK semantics (from the exact `mtk_tinysys_ipi.c` / `mtk-mbox.c` sources)

- **`mtk_ipi_send(dev, id, opt, data, len, timeout_ms)`** is fire-and-forget. `timeout_ms` only bounds the retry loop while the send pin is busy, retrying every 10 µs.
- **`mtk_ipi_send_compl(..., timeout_ms)`** needs both a send pin **and** a recv pin with the same id. It sets `holder`, sends, then waits on the recv pin's `notify` completion: `wait_for_completion_timeout` in WAIT mode, or `mtk_mbox_polling` plus `try_wait_for_completion` every 10 µs in POLLING mode. The deadline is one shared value covering the busy retries and the ACK wait. The reply data lands in the buffer registered with `mtk_ipi_register(dev, id, cb, prdata, msg)`.
  - A recv pin with `recv_opt = ACK (1)` completes only while `holder > 0`. A late ACK after a timeout is dropped.
  - A recv pin with `recv_opt = MSG (0)` completes on every inbound message. `send_compl` still works, but an unsolicited message would also satisfy it. This is the case for every active MCUPM pair except `CH_IPIS_C_MET` (6), including `CH_S_FHCTL` (2) used by `fhctl`. The SCP/VCP sleep and hw-voter pairs use ACK pins.
- **`mtk_ipi_recv`/`mtk_ipi_recv_reply`** wait interruptibly for an inbound MSG pin. `recv_reply` sends the reply on the send pin of the same id, under the pin spinlock.
- The registered callback runs in the mtk-mbox ISR if the recv pin has `cb_ctx = ISR` and `recv_opt = MSG`. With `cb_ctx = process` it only runs inside `mtk_ipi_recv`/`mtk_ipi_recv_reply`. For ACK pins the callback is never called; the reply is just copied into the registered buffer by the ISR. SCP and VCP use 7-cell (`#recv-cells-mode = 1`) and 4-cell recv tables respectively; with 4 cells, `cb_ctx` defaults to 0 = ISR (the `vzalloc`'d table).
